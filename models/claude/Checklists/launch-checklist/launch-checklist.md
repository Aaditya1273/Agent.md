# Purpose

This checklist defines the minimum requirements for successfully launching a software product, feature, platform, API, AI application, SaaS product, developer tool, or enterprise system.

A launch is not the moment software becomes public.

A launch is the coordinated execution of product, engineering, operations, security, marketing, customer success, legal, and business readiness to ensure users experience immediate value with minimal risk.

Successful launches are prepared months before launch day.

# Definition of Done

Launch review is complete when

- Product quality, engineering readiness, operational maturity, and business preparation have all been independently validated.
- Customers can immediately discover, access, understand, and successfully use the product with minimal friction.
- Infrastructure, monitoring, alerting, incident response, and recovery systems provide confidence under expected and unexpected conditions.
- Marketing, sales, customer success, documentation, and support teams are fully aligned with product capabilities and customer expectations.
- Business systems including pricing, billing, analytics, legal, compliance, and reporting operate correctly from the first customer interaction.
- Launch communication clearly explains customer value, product positioning, and available resources without creating misleading expectations.
- Customer feedback, operational metrics, adoption data, and business performance are continuously monitored after launch.
- Risks, limitations, and known issues are documented with mitigation or recovery plans.
- Lessons learned are captured to improve future launches, release quality, and organizational execution.
- The launch establishes long-term customer trust, operational confidence, measurable business value, and a strong foundation for continuous product growth.

An exceptional launch feels effortless.

Customers immediately experience value, systems remain stable under demand, every team understands its responsibilities, issues are detected before they become incidents, and the organization transitions seamlessly from delivering software to supporting a successful product in the real world.
