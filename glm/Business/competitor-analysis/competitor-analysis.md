# Purpose

This document defines how GLM should perform comprehensive competitor analysis for products, startups, SaaS platforms, APIs, developer tools, AI products, consumer applications, marketplaces, and enterprise software.

Competitor analysis is not copying successful companies.

Competitor analysis is the systematic process of understanding the market, identifying opportunities, discovering weaknesses, validating assumptions, predicting future trends, and designing products that create unfair competitive advantages.

The objective is to understand **why competitors win, why they lose, where the market is evolving, and how to build something customers prefer.**

Compete on value.

Never on imitation.

# Definition of Done

Competitor analysis is complete when

- The competitive landscape is clearly defined across direct, indirect, and emerging competitors.
- Customer behavior, purchasing decisions, frustrations, and switching costs are thoroughly understood.
- Products are evaluated across functionality, user experience, pricing, technology, positioning, and business strategy.
- Evidence from customer feedback, reviews, communities, analytics, and market research supports every major conclusion.
- Competitive strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and risks are documented objectively without bias.
- Clear differentiation opportunities identify advantages that competitors cannot easily replicate.
- Strategic recommendations guide product development, pricing, positioning, marketing, and long-term business direction.
- The analysis includes continuous monitoring processes for evolving competitors and market trends.
- Documentation enables founders, product managers, marketers, designers, engineers, and executives to make informed decisions.
- The final outcome transforms competitive knowledge into actionable business strategy rather than simple feature comparison.

Exceptional competitor analysis does not answer,

**"What are competitors building?"**

It answers,

**"What should we build next, why will customers choose it, and how can we create an advantage that remains difficult to copy?"**
